## Answer `F3`

**Item prompt as given to the candidate:**

> A 4-petabyte training corpus. It needs deduplication and a global shuffle, 2,000 GPUs will read it during training, and a run has to be reproducible down to the exact batch six months later. The dedup job currently takes nine days and nobody trusts the shuffle. What breaks first, and what do you fix first?

**Axes to score (only these):** `high_level_design`, `deep_dive_failure_modes`

**Transcript:**

**CANDIDATE:** These are three requirements with three different failure modes, and the nine days is the least urgent of them. Reproducibility is the cheapest and most valuable fix: content-address the shards so they are immutable, then record the manifest, the seed, and the ordering function with the run. That regenerates the exact batch sequence six months later without storing it, and it is days of work, not weeks. Second, the shuffle. A true global shuffle of 4 petabytes means writing 4 petabytes — at even 100 gigabytes a second of sustained I/O that is on the order of 11 hours, so it is not something you do every epoch. I would shuffle once into shards at ingest and do cheap in-shard shuffling plus shard-order randomisation per epoch, which is where the trust problem probably comes from: nobody has measured the resulting distribution. I would add a verification job that samples it. Dedup I would leave at nine days and move it off the critical path, because it runs once per corpus version, not once per run.
